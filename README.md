# Product & Growth Case Studies

I’m Ryan Samuelson, a product leader and hands-on builder who has spent 10+ years working across product, growth, analytics, experimentation, and data.

Most of my professional work has lived inside proprietary systems, so the code and internal artifacts aren’t publicly available.

These case studies focus instead on the parts of the work that matter most:

- What problem were we trying to solve?
- What did the customer or user actually need?
- What did the data tell us?
- What did I decide to build, change, or test?
- How did I think about measuring whether it worked?

Confidential implementation details, internal datasets, screenshots, architecture, and non-public metrics have intentionally been omitted.

### 📈 Galvanize — Redesigning a Complex Customer Journey for Conversion

**Growth Product Management · Customer Research · Funnel Analytics · Experimentation · Information Architecture**

I helped rethink Galvanize.com around the needs of prospective students and customers rather than the company’s internal structure.

The work included customer personas, prospect journeys, information architecture, conversion measurement, experimentation, stakeholder research, and redesigning how users moved from discovery toward application and enrollment.

### 🛒 Campfire — Finding the Growth Levers in an Ecommerce Funnel

**Conversion Analysis · Funnel Diagnosis · Experimentation · Customer Behavior · Growth Strategy**

Given an unfamiliar ecommerce dataset, I analyzed traffic sources, checkout behavior, landing-page performance, and cart abandonment to identify the highest-leverage growth opportunities.

I turned the analysis into a prioritized testing plan rather than stopping at reporting.

*Completed as part of an interview process that resulted in an offer. The original company dataset and materials are not published.*

The work eventually expanded into cross-functional leadership across product managers, program managers, data scientists, data engineers, and developers.

## How I Work

Across very different products and industries, my process tends to look the same:

**Understand the problem → find the signal → form a hypothesis → build or change something → measure what happens → learn and iterate.**

Sometimes that means customer research.

Sometimes it means digging into a funnel.

Sometimes it means building a data product or self-service tool.

And increasingly, it means using AI-assisted development to shorten the distance between an idea and a working product.
