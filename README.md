# Product & Growth Case Studies

I'm Ryan Samuelson, a product leader and hands-on builder with 10+ years working across product, growth, experimentation, analytics, and technical systems.

I've built products inside startups and global technology companies, from conversion experiments and ecommerce experiences to self-service platforms and automated decision systems.

Most of that work lives inside proprietary environments, so these case studies focus on the part I can share: **the problem, how I approached it, the decisions I made, what we built, and what I learned.**

## Case Studies

### 👟 [Nike — Building an Automated Replenishment System for a DTC Supply Chain](./nike-replenishment/)

**Product Strategy · Forecasting · Optimization · Automation · Global Scale**

When Nike's shift toward direct-to-consumer commerce accelerated dramatically in 2020, I took a replenishment product from an early hypothesis and manual planner workflow to an automated decision system that forecasted consumer demand and helped position inventory closer to customers.

The product expanded from a single regional service center across North America and Europe, integrated with existing supply-chain systems, and delivered measurable quarterly EBITDA impact.

**The product problem:** How do you decide where physical inventory should be before the customer orders it?

### 🎓 [Galvanize — Redesigning a Complex Customer Journey for Conversion](./galvanize-growth/)

**Growth PM · Customer Research · Funnel Analytics · Experimentation**

Reframed Galvanize.com around customer intent rather than organizational structure, using customer research, funnel analysis, user testing, and experimentation to redesign journeys across a complex multi-product business.

The work connected acquisition, website behavior, conversion, and downstream enrollment into a measurable growth system.

**The product problem:** How do you help customers navigate a complicated set of choices without making them understand the company first?

---

### 📺 [Prime Video — Turning Operational Complexity Into Self-Service Products](./prime-video-product/)

**Technical PM · Self-Service · Internal Products · Scale**

Built self-service products that helped operations teams investigate and resolve content issues without depending on centralized analysts to answer every question.

The work evolved recurring analytical investigations into reusable product capabilities that could scale with a rapidly growing digital supply chain.

**The product problem:** How do you turn expert knowledge and repeated manual investigation into a product other people can use themselves?

---

### 🛒 [Campfire — Finding the Growth Levers in an Ecommerce Funnel](./campfire-ecommerce-growth/)

**Funnel Analysis · CRO · Experimentation · Prioritization**

Analyzed an unfamiliar ecommerce funnel to understand which acquisition channels and customer journeys were actually creating value, where customers were dropping out, and which changes were worth testing first.

The result was a focused experimentation roadmap built around observed customer behavior rather than a list of generic optimization ideas.

**The product problem:** When a funnel has dozens of things you could improve, how do you figure out what actually deserves attention?

### 🩲 [TomboyX — Diagnosing and Rebuilding an Ecommerce Conversion Experience](./tomboyx-conversion/)

**Ecommerce · CRO · Segmentation · Experimentation · Hands-On Building**

Used customer behavior, site search, segmentation, funnel analysis, and experimentation to identify where an ecommerce experience was losing customer intent.

**Zero-to-One Growth · SEO · Email · Conversion · Monetization**

## The Common Thread

These products look very different.

One moved physical inventory around the world.

One helped prospective students choose an education program.

One turned operational investigations into self-service software.

Others involved ecommerce funnels, customer behavior, community growth, and conversion.

But my approach is usually the same:

**Understand the problem → find the signal → form a hypothesis → build or change something → measure what happens → iterate.**

I tend to be most useful in the messy space between:

> **"We know something isn't working."**

and

> **"Here's what we should build next."**

That's where my background across product, analytics, growth, experimentation, engineering, and operations tends to come together.

## How I Work

I start with the customer or user problem rather than the requested feature.

I use qualitative and quantitative evidence together. Funnel data can tell me **where** something is happening; talking to or observing users often tells me **why**.

I try to make ambiguous problems testable. Sometimes that means an experiment. Sometimes it's a prototype, a dashboard, a manual workflow, or a deliberately limited first version of a much larger product.

I build measurement into the product from the beginning. My analytics background means instrumentation and success metrics aren't something I add after launch.

And I like staying close to implementation. Today that increasingly means using AI-assisted development to shorten the distance between an idea, a working prototype, and something real enough to learn from.
